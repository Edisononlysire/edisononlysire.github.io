Research trajectory

Graduate research began with a concrete gap in cell representation: expression data describe a cell's internal state but cannot, on their own, explain its position and neighbors in tissue. For OmniCell, baseline construction, data analysis, and evaluation tested which tasks benefited from spatial context. Work on GOformer involved designing readouts and comparisons across representation levels to separate what pretraining captured from gains due to downstream optimization. These projects sharpened the question of what information a model actually retains. SPARSE addresses it by adding spatial neighborhoods to frozen transcriptomic embeddings while preserving molecular semantics through expression reconstruction. I led nearly the entire study, from framing and method design to experiments and manuscript preparation.

A clear picture of the current state still leaves open what an intervention will change. Contributions to DynPerturb included data analysis, evaluation, and ablations examining how perturbation timing, intensity, and location affect cell states and regulatory relationships. This work made the link between prediction and evidence more pressing: a plausible result still needs a mechanism that can be explained and independent support that can be checked. The main contribution to BiOmics was the overall research framework, connecting knowledge relations, learned representations, and specialist tools in a traceable reasoning path. For BiOmics Discovery, I led the design of candidate-freezing rules, evidence tiers, and validation paths so contradictory findings could inform the assessment too.

The same evidence problem arises when an agent acts over many steps: new observations arrive, old conclusions may expire, and crucial constraints must remain available. Work on EvoState included the research framing and state mechanisms that distinguish execution traces, past events, and current requirements. The LexStance concept at Peking University Law School focuses on another kind of state: each speaker's position in a multi-party exchange. It proposes a compact, machine-readable record of stances toward a claim so later steps need not repeatedly reinterpret the full dialogue. AEP-ECRG extends the question to active evidence acquisition: its proposed framework weighs the value and cost of further investigation and requires explicit grounds for consequential action.

The thread through these projects is how a judgment can withstand scrutiny. When information is incomplete and goals or conditions change, a recommendation should carry its sources, uncertainty, and unanswered questions. Investigation and action options should be comparable and open to revision as new evidence arrives. The next step is to bring representation, consequence modeling, mechanistic evidence, and task memory into one process so choices in complex settings remain traceable and adjustable.

BGI Beijing Innovation Center · AI Algorithm R&D

OmniCell

Preprint · co-first author

A foundation model for single-cell and spatial transcriptomics

OmniCell addresses the missing spatial context in single-cell transcriptomics and the limited resolution of many spatial assays. Its unified pretraining framework models within-cell gene expression and between-cell spatial dependence; spatially adjacent cells are serialized in the input so that the model can represent genes, cells, and tissue topology together. The paper evaluates cell-type structure, co-expression, deconvolution, and spatial-domain identification.

Contributed to the modeling discussion, built baseline models, performed data analysis and model evaluation, and aligned comparison protocols across tasks. The central question was whether spatial information improved tissue-level interpretation while preserving distinct cellular and molecular features. Read the preprint ↗

BGI Beijing Innovation Center · AI Algorithm R&D

GOformer

Exploratory

Readouts of cell, gene, and Gene Ontology representations

GOformer examines how a pretrained trunk’s cell, gene, and Gene Ontology representations can support cell annotation and transfer. The evaluation separates two questions: a shared linear probe compares the quality of frozen cell representations across models, while a separate optimized readout tests attention aggregation and fusion of gene and GO representations within GOformer. This avoids presenting results from different training protocols as a single ranking.

Helped design the readout and evaluation framework, including controls for cell vectors, gene summaries, GO summaries, and multi-branch fusion, and organized task protocols and result interpretation. The work remains exploratory; no single readout is presented as a final model for every task.

Peking University Law School

LexStance

Concept stage

Stance encoding for multi-party legal dialogue

In a legal scenario with several participants, support, objection, or reservations toward a claim can be scattered across many turns of natural-language dialogue. If an agent retains only the transcript, later steps must reread it and infer each person's position again. LexStance explores compressing the exchange into a machine-readable state record of the speaker, the claim, and the current stance, making positions and their changes easier to track.

Proposed the research question and structured-representation direction. The aim is to make each role's stance clearer while reducing context-token use. The project remains at the concept stage; the encoding format and evaluation plan are still being designed.

State representation · sole first author

SPARSE

ICLR 2027 · under review

Semantic-Preserving Adapter for Robust Spatial Embeddings

SPARSE asks how an existing transcriptomic model can capture tissue structure without retraining its large backbone. Around each focal cell, a lightweight adapter builds a multi-resolution neighborhood using feature-guided token merging, coordinate propagation, spatial rotary position encoding, cross-resolution attention, and top-down fusion. Masked expression reconstruction restrains the adaptation so that spatial context does not erase molecular identity.

The paper evaluates held-out brain regions, an embryo stage, heart and whole-brain sections through spatial-domain identification, cell-type separation, label transfer, and neighborhood-composition prediction, with controlled comparisons to similarly sized adapters. Led nearly the entire project: framed the question and architecture, drove implementation, experiments and ablations, and organized the manuscript and revisions.

Dynamic projection · co-author

DynPerturb

Nature Methods · in revision

Dynamic Perturbation Modeling for Spatiotemporal Single-Cell Systems

DynPerturb models when an intervention occurs, how strong it is, and where it acts in one dynamic framework. Time-indexed graphs represent cells or genes and their regulatory or spatial relations. Temporal encoding, spatial adjacency, and time-stamped memory track signal propagation, regulatory rewiring, and changing cell states; link prediction and node classification provide complementary readouts.

The manuscript examines kidney disease, hematopoietic differentiation, and mouse heart development to study candidate sensitivity windows, lineage transitions, and regional feedback. The documented contribution covers data analysis, model evaluation, ablations, result organization, and manuscript preparation and revision. Biological interpretations are presented as model-supported hypotheses.

Mechanistic reasoning · co-first author

BiOmics

Nature Biotechnology · planned submission

A Foundational Bio-Reasoning Agent for Traceable and Mechanistic Multi-omics Discovery

BiOmics addresses the gap between multi-omics output and biological mechanism. It combines an explicit reasoning space with a latent representation space: a knowledge graph grounds entity relations, modular analytical tools process data and candidates, and an agent plans retrieval and inference. The retrieve–reason–predict workflow is intended to produce hypotheses whose sources and reasoning paths can be inspected. The draft covers indirect pathogenic variants, cell annotation, and drug repurposing as cross-scale evaluation settings.

Primarily designed the research idea and overall framework, including how knowledge, tools, and agent orchestration work together and how to retain provenance, reasoning paths, and hypotheses needing validation. The manuscript is still being revised with co-authors; submission status and co-first authorship follow the author’s supplied information.

Mechanism validation · ongoing

BiOmics Discovery

Exploratory

BiOmics Discovery organizes mechanistic discovery as a reviewable workflow: formulate a biological question, find suitable data, generate candidate mechanisms or targets, freeze the candidate set, then seek independent data, functional evidence, and literature context. In drug-tolerance and oncology settings, the process distinguishes the original discovery from external replication, supplementary support, and contradictory findings rather than counting repeated analysis of one dataset as independent validation.

Led question decomposition, candidate-freezing rules, evidence grading, and validation-path design while supporting integration of data and analytical capabilities. The project remains exploratory, so candidate signals are not presented as confirmed mechanisms or clinical findings.

Persistent decisions · co-first author

EvoState

ICLR 2027 · under review

Multi-Operation Context-State Transitions for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

EvoState studies how long-horizon agents can compress context without losing information needed for the next action. It maintains Current Trace, Event Memory, and Task State as separate records of execution evidence, historical outcomes, and current facts and requirements. A multi-operation editor updates them from new observations; a dependency-aware assembler prioritizes exact identifiers, paths, unresolved requirements, and action dependencies, even when they exceed a soft token target.

The paper compares task success and token use on AppWorld, BFCL Long Context, and an interactive CRMArena-Pro subset, with analyses of reference retention and next-action progress. Helped frame the research idea, three-part state, update mechanism, evaluation, and manuscript argument, shifting the focus from compressing history to maintaining actionable state.

Active evidence · ongoing

AEP-ECRG

Exploratory

AEP-ECRG examines when an agent should investigate further, which evidence it should acquire, and when a response is justified under sparse or adversarial information. The proposed framework uses public observations, current evidence, source trust, and a budget to compare the value and cost of investigative actions. Consequential actions are subject to traceable evidence prerequisites and risk boundaries.

Formulated the research question and overall framework, linking belief updates, active evidence acquisition, and runtime evidence governance, with explicit comparisons of benefit, cost, and risk. Formal training and governance effects remain under evaluation; no unverified performance gains are claimed here.
