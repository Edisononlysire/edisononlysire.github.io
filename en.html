Research trajectory

Graduate research began with a specific question: should cells with similar expression profiles be understood in the same way when they occupy different tissue neighborhoods? In OmniCell, baseline construction, data analysis, and evaluation examined what spatial information contributed to cellular and tissue-level tasks. Work on GOformer involved designing readouts and comparisons for cell, gene, and Gene Ontology representations, separating the quality of pretrained features from gains due to downstream optimization. Together, these studies brought a more fundamental question into focus: how can a model retain both molecular identity and spatial context? For SPARSE, I developed the overall framework, adapted multi-scale ideas from computer vision to spatial-neighborhood modeling, and carried out the downstream evaluations.

Describing a state then raised the question of how an intervention might change it. In DynPerturb, data analysis, model evaluation, and ablations examined the effects of perturbation timing, intensity, and location on cell states and regulatory relationships. Interpreting those results made the need for checkable mechanistic evidence more apparent. The principal contribution to BiOmics was its overall research framework, which connects knowledge relations, latent representations, and specialist tools in a traceable reasoning process. Methodology and analysis for OncoEureka brought together competing oncology hypotheses, prespecified tests, and supporting and contrary findings from distinct data sources, turning preliminary observations into executable plans for the next study.

As the work extended to agents acting over many steps, evidence also had to remain current as a task unfolded. Contributions to EvoState included its research framing and state mechanisms, separating current execution records, past events, and active requirements so new observations do not displace critical constraints. The LexStance concept proposed at Peking University Law School explores a compact, computable record of positions scattered across multi-party dialogue, reducing the need to repeatedly reinterpret the transcript. AEP-ECRG carries the question into active evidence gathering: when existing grounds are insufficient for action, its proposed framework compares the value, cost, and risk of further investigation and sets evidence prerequisites for consequential steps.

Across biological systems and autonomous agents, the recurring question is how a judgment acquires its grounds and remains open to testing as new observations arrive. Future work aims to bring state representation, intervention modeling, mechanistic validation, and task memory into a continuous process. Candidate actions would be presented with their sources, conditions, and unresolved questions, leaving consequential judgments open to review and revision.

OmniCell

Preprint · co-first author

A foundation model for single-cell and spatial transcriptomics

OmniCell addresses the missing spatial context in single-cell transcriptomics and the limited resolution of many spatial assays. Its unified pretraining framework models within-cell gene expression and between-cell spatial dependence; spatially adjacent cells are serialized in the input so that the model can represent genes, cells, and tissue topology together. The paper evaluates cell-type structure, co-expression, deconvolution, and spatial-domain identification.

Built baseline models and evaluation procedures, aligning task settings, controls, and metrics across single-cell and spatial data. Conducted data analysis and model evaluation for cell-type, spatial-domain, and deconvolution tasks, asking whether added neighborhood information improved tissue-level interpretation without obscuring cellular and molecular features. Also contributed to the unified modeling discussion and organization of results. Read the preprint ↗

BGI Beijing Innovation Center · AI Algorithm R&D

GOformer

Exploratory

Readouts of cell, gene, and Gene Ontology representations

GOformer examines how a pretrained trunk’s cell, gene, and Gene Ontology representations can support cell annotation and transfer. The evaluation separates two questions: a shared linear probe compares the quality of frozen cell representations across models, while a separate optimized readout tests attention aggregation and fusion of gene and GO representations within GOformer. This avoids presenting results from different training protocols as a single ranking.

Proposed separating frozen-representation quality from optimized-readout performance. Designed a shared linear-probe comparison and planned controls and ablations for cell vectors, gene summaries, GO summaries, and attention-based fusion. Organized task definitions, training protocols, and interpretation so gains from different settings can be attributed separately. The readout remains exploratory.

Peking University Law School

LexStance

Concept stage

Stance encoding for multi-party legal dialogue

In a legal scenario with several participants, support, objection, or reservations toward a claim can be scattered across many turns of natural-language dialogue. If an agent retains only the transcript, later steps must reread it and infer each person's position again. LexStance explores compressing the exchange into a machine-readable state record of the speaker, the claim, and the current stance, making positions and their changes easier to track.

Proposed a structured speaker–claim–stance record for multi-party dialogue and identified ambiguous positions, stance changes, and conflicting statements as cases the representation must handle. The planned evaluation compares transcript retention with structured state maintenance for stance accuracy and context-token use. The encoding and evaluation are still being designed.

SPARSE

ICLR 2027 · under review

Semantic-Preserving Adapter for Robust Spatial Embeddings

SPARSE asks how an existing transcriptomic model can capture tissue structure without retraining its large backbone. Around each focal cell, a lightweight adapter builds a multi-resolution neighborhood using feature-guided token merging, coordinate propagation, spatial rotary position encoding, cross-resolution attention, and top-down fusion. Masked expression reconstruction restrains the adaptation so that spatial context does not erase molecular identity.

For held-out human-brain regions 1 and 2 and the E16.5 embryo stage, Table 1 reports the highest listed point estimates for both spatial-domain NMI and ARI; the paper also evaluates cell-type separation, label transfer, and neighborhood composition. Contributions included proposing the overall research framework, adapting multi-scale ideas from computer vision to spatial-transcriptomic neighborhoods, and designing the expression-reconstruction constraint to preserve molecular information. I carried out the downstream experiments on spatial-domain identification, cell-type separation, label transfer, and neighborhood composition, including baseline comparisons and ablations, and prepared the figures and manuscript argument.

Dynamic projection · co-author

DynPerturb

Nature Methods · in second-round revision · 2025 IF 28.3

Dynamic Perturbation Modeling for Spatiotemporal Single-Cell Systems

DynPerturb models when an intervention occurs, how strong it is, and where it acts in one dynamic framework. Time-indexed graphs represent cells or genes and their regulatory or spatial relations. Temporal encoding, spatial adjacency, and time-stamped memory track signal propagation, regulatory rewiring, and changing cell states; link prediction and node classification provide complementary readouts.

The manuscript studies kidney disease, hematopoietic differentiation, and cardiac development to examine candidate intervention windows, lineage changes, and regional feedback. As documented in the contribution statement, performed data analysis and model evaluation, helped examine dynamic-relation prediction and downstream performance, conducted ablations, organized comparative results, and contributed to manuscript preparation and the second-round revision. The work tested whether modeled responses to different perturbation settings were stable and interpretable.

Mechanistic reasoning · co-first author

BiOmics

Nature Biotechnology · planned submission · 2025 IF 44.5

A Foundational Bio-Reasoning Agent for Traceable and Mechanistic Multi-omics Discovery

BiOmics addresses the gap between multi-omics output and biological mechanism. It combines an explicit reasoning space with a latent representation space: a knowledge graph grounds entity relations, modular analytical tools process data and candidates, and an agent plans retrieval and inference. The retrieve–reason–predict workflow is intended to produce hypotheses whose sources and reasoning paths can be inspected. The draft covers indirect pathogenic variants, cell annotation, and drug repurposing as cross-scale evaluation settings.

As co-first author, primarily shaped the research idea and overall framework: proposed combining traceable knowledge paths with latent representations; planned the roles and data flow of BiOmics-KG, BRICK tools, and the Agent; and designed a retrieval–analysis–reasoning–prediction workflow that retains sources, intermediate results, and hypotheses requiring validation. Discussed task design, comparison protocols, and the manuscript argument with the team across indirect pathogenic variants, cell annotation, and drug repurposing. The draft remains under joint revision.

Evidence-driven oncology · abstract co-author

OncoEureka

The Lancet Summit · abstract submitted

An evidence-driven AI agent for oncology hypothesis generation and iterative research design

OncoEureka addresses the fragmentation of clinical observations, omics data, and prior evidence in oncology research. It connects question framing, competing mechanistic hypotheses, multisource testing, and the design of the next study. Directional predictions, validation paths, and an analysis plan are specified before candidate-specific testing to limit post hoc selection. The submitted abstract illustrates the process with venetoclax sensitivity in acute myeloid leukemia, comparing discovery, held-out validation, and external evidence, and with cervical-cancer response to concurrent chemoradiotherapy, weighing competing unfolded-protein-response, hypoxia, and EMT–CAF explanations. These findings motivate longitudinal, single-cell, spatial, and functional-intervention follow-up studies.

Contributed methodology and analysis as an abstract co-author, focusing on how candidate hypotheses are fixed before analysis, how supporting and contrary results from distinct sources are compared, and how initial associations become testable research questions. The abstract was submitted to The Lancet Summit: Cancer Control in China ↗ and awaits review.

Persistent decisions · co-first author

EvoState

ICLR 2027 · under review

Multi-Operation Context-State Transitions for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

EvoState studies how long-horizon agents can compress context without losing information needed for the next action. It maintains Current Trace, Event Memory, and Task State as separate records of execution evidence, historical outcomes, and current facts and requirements. A multi-operation editor updates them from new observations; a dependency-aware assembler prioritizes exact identifiers, paths, unresolved requirements, and action dependencies, even when they exceed a soft token target.

Across four settings spanning AppWorld, BFCL Long Context, and a CRMArena-Pro subset, the paper evaluates task success alongside token expenditure. Contributed to the research question and three-part state design, specifying how exact execution references, historical outcomes, current facts, and unresolved requirements should be retained. Helped design multi-operation updates and dependency-aware assembly, including stale-fact invalidation and prioritization of information needed for the next action, and contributed to evaluation design, ablation questions, and the manuscript argument.

Active evidence · ongoing

AEP-ECRG

Exploratory

AEP-ECRG examines when an agent should investigate further, which evidence it should acquire, and when a response is justified under sparse or adversarial information. The proposed framework uses public observations, current evidence, source trust, and a budget to compare the value and cost of investigative actions. Consequential actions are subject to traceable evidence prerequisites and risk boundaries.

Formulated the shift from passive answering to active evidence gathering when evidence is insufficient. Led the proposed division of observations, evidence, source trust, and budget; designed comparisons of investigative value, cost, and risk, together with evidence prerequisites and human-review points for consequential actions. The scenarios and evaluation plan are being specified, and effectiveness remains to be tested.
